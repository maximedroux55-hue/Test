🇨🇭 Rivers as infrastructure, data and urban destiny

Researchers at @EPFL have published work on how rivers shape the growth of cities and the structure of societies, per @EPFL News.

Why it matters:
🌊 Water systems are becoming a measurable input for infrastructure planning, insurance modelling and climate risk pricing, a market that is expanding as cities face flooding and drought in the same decade.
🇨🇭 Switzerland sits at the hydrological heart of Europe and has built world-class expertise in hydrology, remote sensing and environmental modelling, a combination few countries can match at this density.
🏙️ The broader implication: urban planning is shifting from intuition to simulation. That opens space for Swiss spin-offs commercialising environmental data, digital twins and predictive modelling tools for governments and industry.

Academic research like this is often the quiet origin of the DeepTech companies we back five years later.

🇨🇭 Swiss gene editing advances toward in vivo delivery

@Immitra Bio has raised CHF 2.4 million to develop its in vivo gene editing platform, according to @Venturelab Swiss.

Why it matters:
🧬 In vivo editing is the field's central bottleneck: the editing tools work, delivering them safely to the right cells does not, yet. Capital is now concentrating on exactly that problem.
🇨🇭 Switzerland's life sciences density (research institutes, contract manufacturers, regulatory expertise and pharma headquarters within a two-hour radius) lets small teams run serious biology on modest budgets.
💊 If delivery is solved, the addressable set of genetic diseases expands dramatically, and the treatment model shifts from chronic management toward single-administration therapy.

Early-stage biotech in Switzerland tends to be lean by necessity. That discipline often produces sharper milestones and better data per franc spent, which is precisely the profile we look for.
