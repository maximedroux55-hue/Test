🇨🇭 Swiss quantum hardware attracts serious seed capital

@ZuriQ, an @ETH Zurich spinout building trapped-ion quantum computers, has raised a 25.5 million dollar seed round, according to Sifted. It is one of the larger early-stage tickets in European quantum hardware this year.

Why it matters:
⚛️ Seed rounds of this size signal that investors now treat quantum hardware as an engineering roadmap rather than a research bet, and Zurich is on the shortlist of places where that work happens.
🇨🇭 Switzerland's advantage is depth: decades of ion trap and photonics research at ETH Zurich and EPFL, a dense precision manufacturing base, and teams used to doing more with less capital per milestone.
🔭 Scaling qubit counts while holding error rates is the gating factor for real workloads in chemistry, materials and logistics. Every step there pulls in customers, talent and follow-on capital.

Capital-efficient hardware built on genuine scientific edge is exactly the profile we look for at Climb Ventures.

🇨🇭 Gene editing moves inside the body

@Immitra Bio has closed CHF 2.4 million to advance its in-vivo gene editing platform, as reported by Venturelab Swiss. The company is targeting therapies delivered directly in the patient rather than through cells engineered outside the body.

Why it matters:
🧬 In-vivo delivery is the cost problem in cell and gene therapy. Removing the ex-vivo manufacturing step could shift these treatments from specialist centres to standard hospital settings.
🇨🇭 Switzerland pairs world-class life science research with an unusually mature pharma and CDMO cluster, which means a small team can access clinical and regulatory expertise that would take years to assemble elsewhere.
💡 Early rounds of this size are meant to buy validation, not headlines. If the platform holds up in preclinical work, the licensing conversations with larger players tend to follow quickly.

A reminder that Swiss biotech seed cheques stay modest while the underlying science stays ambitious, a combination we find persistently underrated.

🇨🇭 Preventive health scales beyond the Swiss market

@Ahead Health has raised 10 million dollars to launch in Germany and the Netherlands. The Swiss company combines diagnostics and longitudinal data to shift care from treatment towards prevention.

Why it matters:
🩺 European healthcare systems are under cost pressure, and prevention is one of the few levers that improves outcomes and spending at the same time. Consumer willingness to pay is now proven.
🇨🇭 Switzerland is an ideal first market: high health spending per capita, strong diagnostics infrastructure, and demanding customers who expect clinical rigour rather than wellness marketing.
📈 Germany and the Netherlands are the real test. Multi-country expansion forces clarity on regulatory pathways, reimbursement and unit economics, the questions that decide whether this becomes a category or a niche.

Swiss health ventures often prove the model at home before raising to expand, which tends to produce cleaner capital efficiency than the reverse order.

🇨🇭 Swiss cardiac monitoring targets the US market

@Hilo has closed a Series B extension to fund its expansion into the United States. The company develops cuffless blood pressure monitoring, moving hypertension management from occasional readings to continuous data.

Why it matters:
❤️ Hypertension affects more than a billion people and remains poorly controlled. Continuous, comfortable measurement changes what clinicians can actually act on.
🇨🇭 Swiss medtech brings a specific edge: sensor engineering, miniaturisation and a regulatory culture that treats clinical evidence as the product, which is precisely what FDA pathways and US payers demand.
🇺🇸 An extension round rather than a full new raise suggests a company financing a defined commercial step. The US remains the market where medtech valuations are made, and getting reimbursement right there is the hard part.

The pattern is familiar across our ecosystem: rigorous validation first, then measured capital to enter the largest market. Less spectacular than blitzscaling, and considerably more durable.
