🇨🇭 Nanopore sensing takes on lake water safety

Researchers at @EPFL have developed a tiny pore capable of identifying cyanobacteria toxins in lake water. It is a precise answer to a growing problem: warming waters, more frequent algal blooms, and drinking supplies that need continuous monitoring.

Why it matters:
🔬 Environmental diagnostics is shifting from periodic laboratory sampling to distributed, near-real-time sensing, which opens a hardware and data market rather than a services one.
🇨🇭 Switzerland sits at the intersection of world-class nanotechnology, a strong instrumentation industry and thousands of monitored lakes and rivers, which means research and validation environment in the same country.
💧 Single-molecule detection platforms rarely stay in one vertical: the same pore architecture can migrate into clinical diagnostics, food safety and industrial process control.

Sensing technologies out of Swiss labs typically need modest capital to reach a first product, then scale through partners. That profile suits the way we invest.
